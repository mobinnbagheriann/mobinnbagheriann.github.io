Research Interests

How I Arrived Here

I first became interested in physics as a way to understand the universe and reality. Over time, that curiosity moved inward: our only medium for understanding is the brain, so the nature of intelligence became the more fundamental question for me.

In that broader sense, I see AI as one concrete way to study intelligence. By building and analyzing systems, we can ask how simple rules, objectives, and interactions give rise to complex structure and behavior.

This is also why I am drawn to mechanistic interpretability. It offers a way to move from observing intelligent behavior to asking what internal representations and computations make that behavior possible.

Current Interests

Mechanistic interpretability of AI systems

I am interested in understanding what mechanisms emerge inside trained models and how those mechanisms support behavior. I find intervention-based analysis especially compelling because it asks whether a proposed explanation is causally responsible, not only correlated with the output.

Language and multimodal models

LLMs and vision-language models are useful places to study representation, grounding, reasoning, and generalization. My work so far has focused on counting, grounding, binding, and internal representations in these systems.

Emergence in complex systems

I am interested in how simple local rules, interaction, and persistence-like objectives can produce complex structure and behavior. This question connects artificial systems, biological evolution, and learning dynamics.

Artificial life

I am interested in intelligence as a spectrum that is continuous with life, rather than as a sharply separate capacity. From this view, artificial life is a way to ask how adaptive behavior, agency, and intelligence-like structure can arise from simple rules, repeated interaction, and persistence pressures.

Near-Term Direction

More generally, I want to study how simple objectives, learning dynamics, and repeated interactions can produce systems that adapt, persist, and develop increasingly rich behavior.

I see this as connected to mechanistic interpretability. One side studies mechanisms that have already emerged inside large AI systems; the other asks what conditions allow such mechanisms to emerge in the first place.
